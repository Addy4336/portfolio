## The question

Government rules change: loan limits, subsidy amounts, tax rates. A retrieval-augmented
model can have the current rule in its context and still answer with an older one it learned
in training. PolicyDrift measures how often that happens for Indian government schemes, in
English, Hindi and Gujarati, and tests what stops it.

![The PolicyDrift assistant answering a question about PM SVANidhi loans: a "this rule has changed" notice above the answer, the rule version used, and checks that every cited figure appears in an official source](/projects/policydrift/demo-answer.jpg)

## What I built

- **Data:** 46 official releases (PIB and ministries), each recorded with its URL and a
  SHA-256 hash, chunked into 440 passages tagged with the dates their rule version was in
  force.
- **Benchmark:** 42 rule changes across 14 schemes; 85 human-verified items in three
  languages, 255 questions in all. The split is by scheme, and the test split is frozen and
  checksummed behind a loader guard.
- **Experiments:** five retrieval conditions and four mitigations: a date-stamped prompt, a
  validity statement, a conflict-checker agent and version-filtered retrieval. The hypotheses
  and analysis were pre-registered and frozen with a git tag before the test run. Answers are
  scored by a deterministic number matcher plus an LLM judge (κ = 0.83 against 60 stratified
  human labels).
- **The assistant:** FastAPI and Next.js. It applies the two mitigations that worked,
  translates and routes Hindi and Gujarati questions, answers "as of" a chosen date, checks
  that every cited number appears in an official source, flags when the rule has changed, and
  declines out-of-scope questions instead of inventing answers (13 of 13 on a live
  spot-check).

## What the development split shows

These come from the 28-item development split. They shaped the pre-registration, but they
are **not the confirmatory results**: the frozen test split is still being run, and this page
will be updated when it is.

- Models know the old rules: 48–67% of closed-book answers give the outdated value.
- With only the current version retrieved, stale answers drop to 0%. With both versions
  retrieved, they come back (24%).
- In plain RAG, 38 of 40 stale answers came from retrieval missing the current passage, not
  from the model overriding it.
- A one-line date-stamped prompt removed the remaining stale answers when the current rule
  was in context (6.7% → 0%), matching a conflict-checker agent at half the LLM calls.
- Hindi and Gujarati questions were not answered worse than English ones.

## Engineering for reproducibility

A cached, rate-limited LLM client; fact-clustered bootstrap confidence intervals, McNemar
tests and Holm correction; 131 tests and strict mypy.

## Honest limits

- The development results rest on 28 items, and the pre-registered test results are pending.
- Two of the three models share one provider (Gemini). gpt-oss-20b was dropped from the final
  run because of free-tier throughput.
- One person verified the benchmark and labelled the judge sample.
- The hosted demo uses BM25 retrieval, because free hosting cannot load the embedding model;
  the research uses e5-base. On free tiers an answer can take 2–15 seconds.
