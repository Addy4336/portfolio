## The problem

Mock interviews with a chatbot feel like filling in a form. Real interviews are spoken,
they interrupt you, they follow up on the thing you said thirty seconds ago, and they
end. I wanted a practice partner that behaves like an interviewer, not a text box.

## How it works

The browser streams audio over WebRTC through LiveKit. A Pipecat pipeline per interview
runs speech-to-text (Deepgram Flux), decides when you've finished talking, asks an LLM
(Groq `gpt-oss-120b`) for the next move, and speaks it back with Deepgram Aura-2. Every
completed turn is saved, and when you end the session the LLM writes a scored report.

```text
Browser (Next.js 16)  ─►  Supabase  (auth, Postgres with row-level security)
   │   └─► voice-server  POST /spawn  (shared secret)
   └─ WebRTC audio ◄─► LiveKit ◄─► bot.py  (Pipecat, one per interview)

mic ─► STT (Deepgram Flux) ─► turn detection ─► interview conductor
    ─► LLM (Groq gpt-oss-120b) ─► TTS (Deepgram Aura-2) ─► speaker

every turn ─► saved to Postgres        end ─► the LLM writes the scored report
```

## Decisions that mattered

- **A planned interview, not a chatbot.** Left alone, an LLM repeats questions and never
  concludes. The plan lives in code: intro, a deep dive on your own project, a ramp of
  questions from a nine-area bank, your questions, a close. Each turn the model gets a
  short directive and replies with a control tag that is stripped before speech.
- **Turn-taking that waits for you.** Flux detects the end of your turn, and a short,
  cancellable hold means a thinking pause isn't mistaken for a finished answer.
- **A one-second start.** Python imports alone take about 14 seconds, so a dispatcher keeps
  a pre-imported standby bot and hands it the next session.
- **Built for a free tier.** Groq allows about 8k tokens a minute per model, so history is
  trimmed and the bot fails over to a second model on a rate limit.
- **Safe by default.** Row-level security on every table, service-role-only writes for
  transcripts and reports, shared-secret auth on the voice server, LiveKit tokens minted
  only for the caller's own room, and per-account rate limits.

## Honest limitations

Replies take roughly three seconds: end-of-turn detection, a deliberate patience hold,
then the LLM and the first audio. One bot process per interview is fine for a handful of
concurrent sessions, not for scale.

## What I'd change next

- **Close the three-second gap.** The patience hold is tunable; the next step is starting
  speech earlier and measuring each stage of the reply separately.
- **A pool of bots, not one process per interview.** That is the line between "a handful of
  people at once" and anything bigger.
- **Real microphones.** Noise, echo and accents still need human testing; I tuned mostly in
  quiet rooms.
- **Count filler words from the audio.** Speech models drop most "um" and "uh", so the
  count in the report under-reports.
